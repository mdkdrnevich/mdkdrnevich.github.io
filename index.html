01About

I've spent most of my life asking how things work, and then trying to teach computers to find the answers. This December I'm joining the Applied AI team at Everpure, where I'll work with companies to take AI from a promising idea to something that holds up in production.

I spent the last six years on the ATLAS experiment at CERN, building machine learning tools for particle physics simulations: ways to reweight them, check them, and make them far cheaper to run. I did my Ph.D. at NYU with Kyle Cranmer, with a detour to Hamburg as a visiting fellow at Helmholtz AI. Most recently I've been at Root Access, building generative models that speed up 3D electromagnetic simulations.

Before all that, I studied physics and math at Notre Dame, and spent a summer at NASA Glenn building tools for exploring scientific data.

Ask me how physics has guided AI development, where I'm itching to travel next, or where to get the best bowl of ramen in NYC.
